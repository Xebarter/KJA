8. Development priorities
A comprehensive system is best built in stages so that the core academic processes are reliable before advanced features are introduced.
Phase 1: Essential academic operations

Must have

- [ ] Academic years, terms, campuses, classes and subjects
- [ ] Learner and teacher academic profiles
- [ ] Class and subject teacher allocation
- [ ] Timetable management
- [ ] Schemes of work and lesson plans
- [ ] Assessment and examination setup
- [ ] Marks entry and validation
- [ ] Configurable grading and result processing
- [ ] Result approvals and report cards
- [ ] Role permissions and audit logs

Phase 2: Academic control and analysis

High value

- [ ] Curriculum and syllabus coverage tracking
- [ ] Performance analytics and campus comparisons
- [ ] Missing marks and overdue task monitoring
- [ ] Learner interventions and remedial tracking
- [ ] Academic reports and bulk exports
- [ ] Parent portal and result publication
- [ ] Automated reminders and approval workflows
- [ ] Academic calendar and meeting management

Phase 3: Advanced capabilities

Enhancement

- [ ] AI-assisted lesson planning and academic summaries
- [ ] Automated timetable generation
- [ ] Advanced assessment and examination administration
- [ ] Learning resource portal
- [ ] Online assignments and digital assessments
- [ ] Predictive academic risk indicators
- [ ] Advanced curriculum competency analytics
- [ ] Automated academic action summaries


9. What the main dashboard should prioritize
If I were defining the final /academics dashboard, I would make these its most prominent elements, in this order:
1. Academic overview: learners, classes, assessments and current performance.
2. Pending actions: marks awaiting submission, schemes awaiting approval and overdue lesson plans.
3. Academic calendar: upcoming examinations, deadlines and reporting dates.
4. Performance trends: results by campus, class and subject.
5. Learners requiring attention: academic decline, repeated absence and intervention follow-up.
6. Teacher accountability: teaching progress and completion of assigned academic duties.
7. Quick actions: enter marks, create assessment, assign a teacher, review results and generate reports.
8. Recent activity: important academic changes and approvals.

One final architectural recommendation: make the system academic-period-aware from the beginning. Every relevant record should be linked to the correct academic year and term, and finalized results should preserve the grading rules used at the time. This is essential for trustworthy historical reports, promotions, multi-campus comparisons and audit trails.
The result should be an academic command center that helps school leadership monitor the entire teaching and learning process, while allowing teachers to complete their daily work without unnecessary complexity.
